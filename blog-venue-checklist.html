Five things to lock before you book a venue

Lock the date, the guest count and the budget first, and the right venue almost chooses itself.

N
Namratha TejkiranCo-founder · Planning & guest experience · Jul 2026 · 6 min read

Every wedding we have planned in Bengaluru started with the same phone call. Someone has seen a gorgeous venue on Instagram, the family is excited, and there is a quiet panic underneath it all. Where do you even begin?

We will tell you where. Not with the venue. Five things come first, and if you lock them in the right order, the venue almost chooses itself.

This is the checklist we use in our own planning meetings. Save it, argue about it with your family over filter coffee, then come back to it. We have made most of these mistakes ourselves so that you do not have to.

Lock the date, the guest count and the budget first, and the right venue almost chooses itself.

Lock the date and the muhurtham first

Before you look at a single hall, you need a date. In most Bengaluru families that means a muhurtham, and the muhurtham is not negotiable the way a budget is.

Here is the trap we see over and over. A couple falls for a venue, checks availability, and only then discovers the free dates do not match any auspicious window their family priest has given them. Now there is heartbreak, and a scramble to fix something that never needed fixing.

Do it the other way round. Get your muhurtham options first, ideally three or four dates spread across a season, so you have room to move. Bengaluru runs a fierce wedding calendar. The sought-after venues in Malleshwaram, Jayanagar and off Kanakapura Road get booked eight to twelve months ahead for peak dates in the November to February stretch and the April to May window. Leave it late and you are choosing from leftovers.

A quiet word about off-peak dates

If your muhurtham lands on a weekday, or somewhere in the monsoon months, treat it as a gift rather than a compromise. Rates soften. Vendors have time and attention for you. We have run some of our most personal weddings on a Tuesday afternoon in July, and not one guest felt short-changed. When you are ready to tell us your date, the date is the very first thing we ask for.

Lock your real guest count

The second thing to settle is how many people are actually coming. Not the dream number. The real one.

Guest count decides almost everything downstream: the size of the hall, the quantity of food, the parking, the number of coordinators we put on the floor. Get it wrong and you either rattle around a cavernous banquet or fold four hundred people into a room built for two hundred and fifty.

Sit both families down and build one combined list. Then be honest about the Bengaluru factor. The aunt who brings three neighbours. The college friends who RSVP no and turn up anyway. The colleagues invited out of pure courtesy. We usually plan for a footfall a little above the confirmed count, because a half-empty mandap photographs far sadder than a full one. Padding the number a touch also gives your caterer and our floor team breathing room, and nobody remembers a wedding for having slightly too much biryani.

Once you have that number, matching guest count and venue capacity becomes simple arithmetic. A good venue seats your peak comfortably and still has room for a buffet line, a stage and a photo corner. If a hall only fits your crowd shoulder to shoulder, it is too small, whatever the manager tells you. We would rather you book a venue that feels a size too big than one you have already outgrown on paper.

Lock the budget before you fall in love with a venue

Now the unromantic bit. Decide your budget before you visit venues, not after you have lost your heart to one.

The venue is usually the single biggest line in a Bengaluru wedding, and it drags other costs along behind it. A bare shell that looks cheap on paper may need full decor, generator backup, permission for outside catering and a long tail of extras. A slightly pricier all-in venue can quietly work out kinder. Read the full quote twice, then read the footnotes, because that is where the real number hides.

We wrote a whole piece on where your budget actually goes, and the short version is this: the sticker price of the hall is rarely the price you pay.

What quietly eats your venue budget

Watch for these: rental capped at a fixed number of hours with steep overtime, mandatory in-house caterers, royalty or corkage on outside food, a separate charge for air-conditioning, valet parking, and refundable cleaning deposits that are not always refunded. When we run in-house catering ourselves, a lot of these grey charges simply vanish, because there is no third party adding a tax at every step. Fix a number, set aside ten percent for the surprises, and hold the line even when the marble lobby is whispering sweet nothings at you.

The quick version

  • Get your muhurtham dates from your family priest before you tour a single venue; the date leads, the venue follows.
  • Build one honest guest list across both families and plan for a footfall slightly above your confirmed count.
  • Set your budget before you fall for a hall, and keep ten percent aside for the extras that never make the sticker price.
  • Check parking, power backup, green rooms and the hard stop time; the pretty photos hide the logistics that matter most.
  • Ask the boring questions in writing before you sign; a confident venue will put every answer on paper.

Lock the logistics you cannot see on a site visit

A venue can look glorious at four on a dry Sunday afternoon and behave like a different place on your actual wedding day. So look past the pretty.

Parking is the one nobody checks until it is far too late. In neighbourhoods like Indiranagar or Basavanagudi, street parking on a busy evening is pure fiction. Ask how many cars the venue holds, and what happens to the overflow when your two hundredth guest arrives.

Then weigh the things a photograph will never show you. Is there a clean, close green room for the bride and groom to change and rest? Does the power backup carry the whole event, or only the lights when the mains drop? Can the caterers actually cook in the kitchen provided? Is there step-free access for your grandparents? And what is the hard stop time, because plenty of Bengaluru venues enforce a curfew to the minute and will pull the plug mid-song.

We walk every venue at roughly the hour your event will run, because light, traffic and temperature all shift through the day. This is exactly where end-to-end coordination earns its keep: the plan has to survive contact with a real Saturday. By the time the mandap is lit, we already know where the sun sits and which gate the cars should use.

Lock the questions you must ask before signing

By now you have a date, a headcount, a budget and a feel for the space. Before you sign anything, ask the boring questions. The boring questions are the ones that save weddings. Weddings rarely fall apart over the grand things. They wobble over the small print nobody read.

  • What exactly is included in the quoted price, and what is billed on top?
  • How many hours do we get, and what does overtime cost per hour?
  • Can we bring our own decor, caterer and film crew, or are there in-house vendors we are forced to use?
  • What is the cancellation and postponement policy, in writing?
  • Is there a backup plan for rain, a power cut or a double booking?
  • Who is our single point of contact on the day itself?

These are the essential questions to ask before booking a venue, and a confident venue will happily put every answer on paper. If someone dances around them, treat that as your answer. We would far rather you walk away now than argue over a surprise invoice the week of the wedding.

Lock it in, then breathe

Notice what we did not do. We did not open with fairy lights, a colour palette or a clever hashtag. We locked the date, the guest count, the budget, the logistics and the fine print, in that order. The venue then becomes a decision you make with a clear head, not a crush you talk yourself into and regret. You will visit halls that dazzle you and halls that leave you cold, and this order keeps you steady through both.

That, honestly, is how to choose a wedding venue in Bengaluru without lying awake at night. Do the unglamorous work first, and the beautiful part gets easier and a great deal more fun.

And if it still feels like a mountain, that is precisely our job. We plan the whole wedding in-house, from the first venue walk to the final goodbye, so you spend the run-up excited rather than exhausted. Creativity is our credibility, and for us it starts with getting these five boring, brilliant things right.

What should I lock before booking a wedding venue in Bengaluru?

+

Lock five things first: your muhurtham date, your real guest count, your budget, the logistics like parking and power, and the fine print in the contract. Sort these in that order and the venue decision becomes straightforward. The venue should be the result of your planning, not the start of it.

How far in advance should I book a wedding venue in Bengaluru?

+

For peak dates between November and February or April and May, book eight to twelve months ahead, because the popular venues fill fast. Off-peak weekdays and monsoon dates are far easier and often cheaper. Once you have your muhurtham options, start venue hunting straight away.

How do I match my guest count to venue capacity?

+

Build one combined guest list, then plan for a footfall slightly higher than your confirmed number. Choose a venue that seats that peak comfortably with room for a buffet, a stage and a photo corner, not one that only fits your crowd shoulder to shoulder. If a hall feels tight on paper, it will feel tighter on the day.

What questions should I ask before booking a venue?

+

Ask what is included versus billed on top, how many hours you get and the overtime rate, whether you can bring your own decor, caterer and film crew, the cancellation policy, the backup plan for rain or power cuts, and who your point of contact is on the day. Get every answer in writing. A venue that dodges these questions has already told you what you need to know.
