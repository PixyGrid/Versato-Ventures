Budgeting

Where your wedding budget actually goes

Guest list discipline is the single most powerful budgeting tool you own.

S
Sourabh KulkarniCo-founder · Creative direction & execution · May 2026 · 7 min read

Let us start with the honest bit. Most families do not overspend on a wedding because they are careless. They overspend because nobody told them where the money actually goes, and by the time they find out, three vendors have already been booked.

We build clear itemised budgets for a living, so we see the same surprises again and again. The big numbers are rarely the shock. It is the small, quiet line items that pile up while you are looking the other way.

So here is a plain wedding budget breakdown in Bengaluru, written the way we would explain it to you across a table, with no fluffy averages we cannot back up.

Guest list discipline is the single most powerful budgeting tool you own.

Catering and venue: the two that take the biggest bite

If you remember one thing, remember this: catering and venue usually swallow the largest share of any wedding, often more than half of the total between them. Everything else competes for what is left.

Catering scales with every name on your list

Food is priced per head, which makes it the most ruthless line item you have. Your catering and cuisine cost is a simple sum: catering cost per plate multiplied by your guest count, then multiplied again by the number of meals and counters you commit to. Add a live pasta station here, a chaat corner there, a midnight snack for the sangeet, and the per plate figure quietly climbs.

The lever you control is not the menu. It is the headcount. Cutting fifty guests saves you far more than dropping a dessert. We say this often and we mean it: guest list discipline is the single most powerful budgeting tool you own.

Venue sets the ceiling for everything else

Your venue does more than cost money. It decides how much everything around it will cost. A raw banquet with bare walls needs a lot of decor to feel like an occasion. A garden or heritage space that already looks beautiful can halve your decor bill. Rental, and whether the venue forces its own caterer on you, matters as much as the sticker price.

Decor: where taste quietly meets maths

Decor is the most emotional spend on the list, which is exactly why it needs a firm hand. A decor and design plan can be modest or it can rival your catering, and the difference is usually scale, not beauty.

Your wedding decor budget tends to sit as a meaningful chunk of the total, and it moves with three things: the size of the space you are dressing, the amount of fresh flower you use, and how many separate setups you need. Three functions in three locations means three builds, three teardowns, three logistics runs. That is where the number grows.

Our honest advice: pick two or three moments to spend on, the mandap, the entrance, the main stage, and let the rest stay simple. A room does not need to be full to feel considered. It needs to be intentional.

Coordination and crew: the cost that pays for itself

This is the line item families are quickest to question and quickest to thank us for later. Coordination is not an add-on. It is the reason your caterer, decorator, priest and photographer all arrive, set up and finish in the right order without you fielding forty phone calls in your wedding clothes.

Beyond our fee, there is a crew nobody budgets for: helpers, ushers, technicians, cleaners, the people who run the day on the ground. Sound and lighting, generators, and backup power sit here too. None of it is glamorous. All of it is the difference between a day that runs and a day that limps. When this line is underfunded, it does not show in the plan. It shows on the day, which is the worst possible time to find out.

The quick version

  • Catering and venue usually take the biggest share, often more than half between them. Start your budget here, not with the pretty stuff.
  • Guest count is your strongest lever. Cutting names saves far more than cutting dishes.
  • Decor scales with space, fresh flower and the number of separate setups. Spend on two or three moments and keep the rest simple.
  • Hidden costs, taxes, logistics, overtime and gratuities, can add ten to fifteen percent. Always ask whether a quote is tax inclusive.
  • Keep a five to ten percent buffer untouched. You will very likely use some of it.

Photography, film, attire and the keepsakes

These are the spends you carry home. Photography and film hold their value because they are the only part of the day you actually get to keep, and good crews book out early, so they are rarely the place to cut hard. Attire and jewellery sit in a similar bracket: deeply personal, easy to overspend on, worth setting a firm figure before you step into a single showroom.

Our steer here is simple. Decide early whether memory or spectacle matters more to you, because that choice quietly sets your priorities. Some families would rather have a smaller decor spend and a photographer they love. Others feel the opposite. Both are right. What is not right is discovering the trade off after both are already booked.

The costs families almost always underestimate

Here is where the hidden wedding costs live. Individually they look small. Together they can add ten to fifteen percent you never planned for.

Taxes, service charges and gratuities

Quotes are often shown before tax. GST and service charges can lift a figure noticeably, and tips for a large crew add up faster than you expect. Always ask whether a number is inclusive or not. We put this in writing so there are no raised eyebrows on settlement day.

Logistics, overtime and the in-between

Transport, guest accommodation, travel for the crew, and overtime if your function runs long past its slot. Print and stationery, favours, cloakroom and washroom upkeep, trial runs and tastings. None of these headline the budget. All of them appear on the final bill.

The buffer you will be glad you kept

Weather changes. A guest count creeps up. A supplier falls through and the replacement costs more. Keep roughly five to ten percent aside and untouched. If you do not need it, wonderful. You almost always need some of it.

How the money typically splits, and how to protect it

People ask us how much does a wedding cost in Bengaluru as if there is one answer. There is not. There is only your answer, decided by guest count, number of functions and the standard you want to hold. What we can give you is a sensible pattern for wedding budget allocation.

As a rough guide for the proportions we see: catering and venue together take the largest share, decor a substantial slice, then photography and film, attire and jewellery, coordination and crew, and finally that buffer. Entertainment, invitations and gifting fill the gaps. Your split will lean differently, and that is the point. Decide what matters most to you, fund it properly, and trim honestly everywhere else.

To protect the budget: set the guest list before anything else, get every quote itemised and tax inclusive, and put changes in writing as they happen. Planning a full wedding works best when the number is a shared, visible document, not a running total in someone's head. If you want a head start, get an itemised budget from us and we will build it around your guest count and your priorities.

What usually takes the biggest share of a wedding budget in Bengaluru?

+

Catering and venue almost always lead, frequently more than half of the total between them. Food is priced per plate, so it scales with every guest, and the venue quietly sets how much decor and logistics will cost around it. Start your budget with these two before anything else.

How do I estimate catering cost per plate?

+

Take a realistic per plate figure from your caterer, multiply it by your final guest count, then account for every extra meal and counter across your functions. Additional live stations raise the effective per head cost more than most people expect. The fastest way to control it is to control the guest list, not the menu.

What are the hidden wedding costs people forget?

+

Taxes and service charges, gratuities for a large crew, transport and accommodation, overtime if functions overrun, plus stationery, favours and trial tastings. On their own they look minor, but together they can add ten to fifteen percent. Ask whether every quote is inclusive of tax before you compare numbers.

How much should I keep as a buffer?

+

We suggest keeping roughly five to ten percent of your total aside and untouched. Guest counts creep, weather turns, and a supplier occasionally falls through at the last minute. If you finish without needing it, that is simply money back in your pocket.
